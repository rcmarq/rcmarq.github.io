Research

Science!

I work at the intersection of different fields that span Meteoritics and Cosmochemistry, Planetary Science, Geochronology, Paleoceanography and most recently, Paleoclimate. A central theme through my excursions in these seemingly disparate fields is a special attention to high-precision techniques in Isotope Geochemistry. I outline some of my past and ongoing projects below. Feel free to get in touch if you are interested in collaborating or just want to talk science!

Ongoing Project

Volatile Extinct Radionuclides and the Origins of Lunar Volatile Depletion

We have identified signatures consistent with the incorporation of extinct 135Cs in secondary phases of fine-grained CAIs, with an initial abundance orders of magnitude higher than estimates from inner Solar System meteorites. This hints at a spatial dichotomy in the distribution of 135Cs — in stark contrast to the relative homogeneity of other short-lived radionuclides such as 26Al.

In collaboration with Dr. Nicole Nie (MIT), we are leveraging the radiogenic decay of 135Cs (t1/2 = 2.30 Myr) into 135Ba to probe the timing of volatile depletion in lunar rocks. A key question is whether the depletion signature seen in the Moon was inherited from a volatile-depleted impactor, rather than being solely the product of the giant impact itself.

Ongoing Project

The Barium Isotope Dichotomy of Meteorites

I have compiled barium isotope anomaly data from bulk meteorites that reveal a previously unrecognized dichotomy between carbonaceous (CC) and non-carbonaceous (NC) reservoirs. A distinctive feature of the Ba system is the asymmetric offset between NC and CC end-members when comparing anomalies in 135Ba — potentially linked to the radiogenic decay of 135Cs — which may allow us to constrain both the timing of the NC/CC dichotomy and the relationship between volatile depletion and the spatial segregation of early planetary bodies.

In collaboration with Dr. Meenakshi Wadhwa (ASU), I am developing new measurement protocols using the next-generation Thermo Fisher Neoma MC-ICPMS to improve the precision of these bulk meteorite analyses.

Ongoing Collaboration

Presolar Carriers of Isotope Anomalies in Meteorites

The isotopic dichotomy between carbonaceous and non-carbonaceous meteorites is well established, but the identity of the presolar grains responsible for these signatures remains uncertain. In collaboration with Dr. Larry Nittler (ASU) and using a modified duoplasmatron NanoSIMS source developed by Dr. Peter Williams (ASU), we are measuring Fe, Cr, Ti, and Ni isotope anomalies in individual oxide grains from chondritic meteorites to identify the true stellar sources of the r-process excess that defines the CC/NC divide.

Ongoing Method

A Mathematical Re-interpretation of Short-Lived Radionuclide Isochrons

Short-lived radionuclide isochrons are among the most powerful tools for dating early Solar System events, but their interpretation conflates temporal and petrological information in ways that are rarely made explicit. I have developed a quantitative framework for disentangling these two contributions, with direct applications to constraining the timing of aqueous alteration in meteorites and the formation ages of chondrules.

Published Project

Stardust as Seeds for Nebular Condensation

It has long been assumed that presolar grains would not have survived the extreme temperatures of the CAI-forming region. The highly anomalous nucleosynthetic Sr signatures I measured in refractory leachates from CAIs challenge this notion. Using ultra-low blank chromatography and a new analytical scheme I developed called "intra-sample addition," I was able to indirectly resolve isotope anomalies at picogram to sub-picogram levels of Sr — pushing the boundary of what is analytically accessible.

Published Project

Stellar Sources of Early Solar System Building Blocks

Using step-leaching, I analyzed the nucleosynthetic Ba isotope compositions of fine-grained CAI components. The primary and secondary phases of these refractory inclusions reflect multiple episodes of re-condensation in isotopically distinct reservoirs, tracing the contribution of various stellar nucleosynthetic sources and the transport and processing of the earliest Solar System condensates.

This work was recognized with the 2024 Pellas-Ryder Award by the Meteoritical Society and the GSA Planetary Division.

Published Method

Methods for Sample-Limited Isotope Analyses

Analyzing individual components of CAIs — already small at the bulk level — demands techniques that push the limits of sensitivity and contamination control. My graduate work resulted in COSMO, an open-source tool for optimizing the double spike technique for extraterrestrial samples, as well as established protocols for ultra-low blank chromatography (<1 pg Sr & Ba background) and analysis using 1013Ω amplifiers.

In progress Collaboration

Paleoclimate: Death Valley and the 41-kyr World

In collaboration with Dr. Elizabeth Niespolo (Princeton), I am working to secure funding to study saltpan sediments from the Confidence Hills Formation in Death Valley, CA. The goal is to test whether this section preserves a paleoclimatic response to precessional (23-kyr) forcing during the Plio-Pleistocene — a period (3.0–0.8 Ma) when 23-kyr power is conspicuously absent from the global climate record despite predictions from orbital theory.

I am also interested in using barium isotopes in deep-sea archives to track past changes in primary productivity and nutrient cycling, and their role in driving glacial-interglacial cycles.

In progress Collaboration

D/H Signatures and Water Incorporation in Perovskites

In collaboration with Dr. William Palfey (Carnegie Institution) and Dr. Maitrayee Bose (ASU), we are exploring D/H isotopic ratios as tracers for how hydride (H−) is incorporated into perovskite structures (ABX3). These minerals are present in both early Solar System condensates and the deep Earth, making this tracer potentially relevant for understanding how hydrogen — and by extension water — is acquired and preserved across planetary bodies.
