The Early Solar System as told by its First Condensates

My work in graduate school focused primarily on fine-grained Calcium-Aluminum-rich Inclusions (CAIs). Fine-grained CAIs are nebular condensates that have not seen significant melting since forming, unlike their coarse-grained counterparts. This means that they are tremendous records of early Solar System processes. They do however present a significant analytical challenge due to the lower concentrations of some of elements of interest, as well as potential contamination issues that come from their porous textures. This demands a complete re-vamping of isotopic techniques in order to get resolvable signals (see section on Sample-limited Analyses).

Using step-leaching, I analyzed the nucleosynthetic Ba isotope compositions of fine-grained CAI components. The primary and secondary phases of these refractory inclusions reflect multiple episodes of re-condensation in isotopically distinct reservoirs, tracing the contribution of various stellar nucleosynthetic sources and the transport and processing of the earliest Solar System condensates. This work was recognized with the 2024 Pellas-Ryder Award by the Meteoritical Society and the GSA Planetary Division.

Another paper from my PhD thesis is currently in review that details how presolar nucleation seeds impart isotope anomalies to these nebular condensates. Check back soon for updates on this work!
